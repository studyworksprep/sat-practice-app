## The four decisions that apply to every lesson

### End on the item, not the recital

Today 32 of 36 lessons end retrieval-check → lesson_complete, with the SAT-format item one step earlier. The voice guide says end hard: the last thing a learner does is the "you vs. the SAT" item. Proposal: swap the final two activities so the order is retrieval check → authentic item → sign-off. Already the case in Find Missing Constants; CLEAR and Sliders end on a bank question (fine); Good Cop / Bad Cop ends on its own paired checks (leave). If the team would rather keep "say the routine back" as the final beat, say so — this is one decision for all 32.

### Drop the three "in one glance" summary blocks

My Numbers, Rhetorical Synthesis, and Odd-One-Out each carry a text block ("My Numbers in one glance", "The strategy in one glance", "The shortcut in one glance") between the retrieval check and the sign-off. It is the summary card the voice retires, and the retrieval check already asks for the routine. Proposal: delete all three.

### Delete the twelve "Next, you will…" tails

Mechanical, no judgment needed: the sentence goes, the explanation keeps its answer. Listed at the end so the team can see them; applied with the rest on approval. The linter (lint_retired_tail) keeps them from coming back.

### Explanation openers are a follow-up, not this pass

587 of 625 explanations (94%) open "Correct." / "Right." / "Exactly." That is the cadence the guide now retires, but varying 587 lines is a voice pass in its own right, and one the tutors should read rather than approve blind. The corpus report now shows each lesson's same-word-opener rate; the generator and skill write new lessons varied. Proposal: schedule it as 6.2b after names are settled, so the rewrites can use the names.

### Advanced Factoring: Non-Monic Trinomials and Cubes

**Handle:** Outside-Inside Check *(proposed)* — The check that decides every trial: the outside and inside products must add to the middle term.

**Opener** (block 1, below the card)

Was:

> You already know that factoring reverses multiplication. In this lesson, you will use that same idea to:
>
> - factor trinomials such as \(6x^2+11x+3\), whose leading coefficient is not \(1\);
> - recognize when a non-monic trinomial is prime over the integers;
> - derive and use the sum- and difference-of-cubes identities;
> - combine GCF, trinomial, square, and cube methods in complete factorizations.
>
> > **No new trinomial trick:** non-monic factoring will be organized trial and error. Every trial comes from the multiplication you are trying to reverse.

Proposed:

> A leading coefficient that isn't \(1\) stops most people cold: \(6x^2+11x+3\) doesn't split by the sum-and-product habit. It still factors — by organized trial, checked by one number.
>
> > **No new trick:** every trial comes from the multiplication you're reversing, and the middle term tells you whether it worked.

**Closer** (`lesson_complete`)

Was:

> **You can factor advanced polynomial forms**
>
> You can organize non-monic trials from factor pairs, use cross-products to accept or reject an arrangement, prove when no integer factorization exists, derive the cube identities from cancellation, and combine those methods with GCF and difference of squares.

Proposed:

> **The middle term decides it**
>
> Non-monic trinomial, cube, or a mix of both — the outside-inside products have to add to the middle term, and the cube identities come back from cancellation whenever you forget them. Before you grind, ask whether the question wanted a factored form at all.
