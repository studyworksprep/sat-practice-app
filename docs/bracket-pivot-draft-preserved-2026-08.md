<h2>Make the logic small before the choices make it noisy</h2><p>Transition questions can bury a simple relationship inside unfamiliar research, history, or art. Your job is not to become an expert on the topic. Your job is to reduce two ideas to a tiny logic map and choose the transition whose exact meaning matches it.</p><blockquote><p><strong>Goal:</strong> Bracket the pivot, translate both sides, predict the relationship, and match the most precise transition - including questions built around a local signal such as <em>rarely</em>, <em>instead</em>, or <em>next</em>.</p></blockquote>

## 2. Alternative opening exploration (street-tree example; the spec uses a restoration example)

Text: "Researchers found that shade from street trees lowers sidewalk
temperatures during the hottest part of the day. _____ several cities
seeking to reduce heat exposure have expanded tree-planting programs."
Choices: Moreover, / Therefore, (key) / For example, / Nevertheless,
Hint: "Ask whether the finding merely agrees with the cities' action or
gives them a reason to take it."
Explanation: "Yes. The finding gives cities a reason to expand tree
planting, so the second idea follows from the first. Therefore signals
that result or warranted response; the ideas do not merely agree."
